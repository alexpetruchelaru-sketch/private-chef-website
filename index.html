Huerta

Menú de mercado · Cuatro platos

A menu built on whatever the market gave up that morning. Aubergines burnt whole over the flame until they collapse into smoke, tuna cut to order, black pig raised on the island and cooked until the skin cracks under the knife. It ends with caramel and honey rum, poured at the table while it is still too hot to eat.

  1. Para empezar

    Berenjena a la brasa, cebolla, pan

    Charred aubergine, onion, bread

    Burnt whole over the flame until they collapse. Deep smoke, slow onion, and bread still warm enough to steam when it is torn.

  2. Primero

    Atún, ponzu, aguacate

    Tuna, ponzu, avocado

    Cut to order and dressed at the last moment, so the ponzu arrives late and sharp rather than settling in.

  3. Principal

    Panceta de cerdo negro canario, manzana, zanahoria

    Canarian black pork belly, apple, carrot

    Black pig raised on the island. Skin that cracks under the knife, and apple sharp enough to cut straight through it.

  4. Alternativa

    Cherne, mojo verde, papa antigua

    Stone bass, green mojo, heritage potato

    Skin crisped hard and dry, green mojo beneath, heritage potatoes crushed alongside.

  5. Postre

    Torrija de plátano, brûlée, ron miel

    Plantain custard French toast, honey rum

    Soaked overnight in a custard of island plantain, with plantain caramelised to a brûlée crust across the top that cracks under the spoon.

Travesía

Menú degustación · Cuatro platos

A crossing. Fish line-caught off Arguineguín and cured at first light, served cold in a clear tomato dashi. Brown butter turned savoury with miso. Kid goat given a whole day, then glazed dark with palm honey and star anise until the edges catch, its sauce drawn from the bones. It closes with a cheesecake made from a cheese set by thistle flower rather than rennet.

  1. Para empezar

    Vieja curada, dashi de tomate, pepino, mojo verde

    Cured parrotfish, tomato dashi, cucumber, green mojo

    Line-caught off Arguineguín and cured at first light. Cold, clear, and green with coriander at the finish.

  2. Primero

    Vieira, coliflor, beurre noisette de miso, lima

    Scallop, cauliflower, miso brown butter, lime

    Caught on one side only and left barely warm through. Brown butter turned savoury with miso, lifted at the last second by lime.

  3. Principal

    Baifo, miel de palma, anís estrellado, papa antigua

    Kid goat, palm honey, star anise, heritage potato

    A whole day of cooking, then glazed dark with palm honey from La Gomera until the edges catch. The sauce comes from the bones.

  4. Alternativa

    Cherne, mojo verde, papa antigua

    Stone bass, green mojo, heritage potato

    Skin crisped hard and dry, green mojo beneath, heritage potatoes crushed alongside.

  5. Postre

    Queso de Guía, higo, miel

    Flor de Guía cheesecake, fig, honey

    Made with the cheese set by thistle flower rather than rennet, faintly grassy behind the cream. Black figs roasted in honey.

Sobremesa

Menú de celebración · Cuatro platos

Named for the hour after the plates are cleared. Prawn on focaccia, glass-crisp beneath and soft above, with black garlic and a chilli oil that arrives a second late. Beef cut by hand beneath a yolk cured until it grates. Suckling pig confited six hours, then crisped so the skin shatters under a spoon. It ends with island cheeses, quince and gofio, deliberately, so the evening has somewhere left to go.

  1. Para empezar

    Gamba sobre focaccia, ajo negro, guindilla

    Prawn on focaccia, black garlic, chilli

    Glass-crisp underneath, soft above. Black garlic, sweet and almost balsamic, and a chilli oil that arrives a second after everything else.

  2. Primero

    Tartar de ternera, yema curada

    Beef tartare, cured yolk

    Cut by hand and dressed lightly, under a yolk cured in salt until it grates over the top like a hard cheese.

  3. Principal

    Cochinillo confitado, papa antigua, manzana, tomillo

    Confit suckling pig, heritage potato, apple, thyme

    Six hours in its own fat, then taken to a fierce heat so the skin shatters under the back of a spoon. Heritage potatoes, roast apple, thyme picked that morning.

  4. Alternativa

    Solomillo Wellington, zanahoria, Madeira, trufa

    Beef Wellington, heritage carrot, Madeira, truffle

    Fillet and mushroom in pastry, carved at the table, with glazed heritage carrots and a Madeira and truffle sauce.

  5. Postre

    Quesos canarios, membrillo, gofio

    Canarian cheeses, quince, gofio

    Three island cheeses at three ages, fresh, semi-cured and aged, with quince and a gofio biscuit. The course nobody gets up from.

Brasa

Menú a la brasa · Mínimo seis personas

Everything over coals, brought to the table as it comes off the fire rather than plated in courses. Chicken two days in lemon and oregano, lamb and beef worked together with cumin and mint, pork ribs overnight in gochujang. A salad table that stays out all afternoon and gets refilled without anyone asking.

  1. De la brasa

    Souvlaki de pollo, limón, orégano

    Marinated chicken souvlaki

    Two days in lemon, oregano and garlic before it ever meets the fire. Off the skewer at the grill, straight to the table.

  2. De la brasa

    Kofta de cordero y ternera, comino, menta

    Spiced lamb and beef kofta

    Lamb and beef worked together with cumin, mint and chilli, charred hard on the outside and left pink within.

  3. De la brasa

    Costillar de cerdo, gochujang

    Korean-marinated pork ribs

    Overnight in gochujang, ginger and soy, cooked low over the coals until the meat draws back from the bone, then glazed hard at the end.

  4. De la brasa

    Hamburguesa de la casa

    Homemade burger patty

    Ground and pressed the same morning, seared on the plancha, for the children and for anyone who wants one thing they recognise.

  5. De las brasas

    Maíz a la brasa, guindilla, lima

    Grilled corn, chilli and lime

    Charred in the husk until it blackens, then rolled in chilli butter and lime while still too hot to hold.

  6. De la mesa

    Ensaladas y salsas de la casa

    Salad table and sauces

    Leaves, tomato, herbs and grains, with chimichurri, garlic yoghurt and a chilli sauce. Left on the table all afternoon, and refilled without being asked.

  7. Para terminar

    Brochetas de fruta de temporada

    Seasonal fruit skewers

    Whatever the market had that morning, turned briefly over the last of the heat so the sugars catch.

Media Mañana

Desayuno tardío · A elegir uno

The meal that starts late and is in no hurry. Bread, fruit, juice and coffee are on the table before anyone sits down, and stay there. Then one plate each, cooked to order: Turkish eggs under warm harissa oil, hollandaise made at the moment and never held, pancakes brought out in twos so the last is as warm as the first.

  1. Para la mesa

    Pan artesano, fruta, zumo, café

    Artisan bread, fruit, juice, coffee

    On the table before anyone sits, and there for as long as they want it.

  2. A elegir

    Huevos turcos, aceite de harissa, pan artesano

    Turkish eggs, warm harissa oil, artisan bread

    Poached eggs over cold garlic yoghurt with a warm harissa oil poured across at the last moment, so it pools and separates. Bread for the rest.

  3. A elegir

    Aguacate, huevo escalfado, semillas, aceite virgen

    Avocado, poached egg, seeds, virgin olive oil

    Avocado crushed rather than sliced, toasted seeds for texture, and a green olive oil poured with a heavy hand.

  4. A elegir

    Huevos benedictinos, salmón, panceta o espinacas

    Eggs benedict, smoked salmon, pancetta or sautéed spinach

    On artisan bread rather than muffin, with hollandaise made to order and never held. Choose smoked salmon, crisp pancetta, or baby spinach wilted in butter.

  5. A elegir

    Torrija, plátano caramelizado, mascarpone, sirope de arce

    French toast, caramelised plantain, whipped mascarpone, maple

    Plantain caramelised in the pan until the edges darken, with mascarpone whipped loose and maple poured warm.

  6. A elegir

    Tortitas de suero, mascarpone, sirope de arce

    Buttermilk pancakes, whipped mascarpone, maple

    Thick, slow on the pan, and brought out in twos so the last one is as warm as the first.

The chef

I grew up on a farm. That is still the part that matters most.

I learned what a tomato tastes like when it is picked at the right hour, and how an animal should be raised, long before I learned to cook one properly.

The technique came later, over a decade in Cambridge’s best kitchens: the University Arms, Autograph Collection by Marriott; DoubleTree by Hilton; Quy Mill. What I cook now sits between the farm and that training. French and English method, Asian flavour, and whatever the island gave up that morning. Cherne with green chilli and coriander mojo. Mojo picón with papas antiguas. Baifo, the island kid goat, slow cooked with palm honey and star anise. Local prawns with XO butter and gofio. Vieja line-caught off Arguineguín and cured the same morning. I cook it in villas and homes across Maspalomas, Meloneras, Mogán, Puerto Rico, Playa del Inglés and Las Palmas de Gran Canaria.

I write every menu for the table that booked it. Vegetarian guests get their own three courses, not the side dishes. Tell me you hate coriander and you will never see it.

I arrive two hours before service with everything, including my knives. I only need your oven and two burners. I cook in your kitchen, serve each course at your table, and leave it cleaner than I found it. You do not shop, plate, pour or wash up.

How an evening works

You do not shop, plate, pour or wash up

  1. I shop that morning

    The market first, then the fish counter. The menu is finished after I have seen what is actually there.

  2. I arrive two hours early

    With everything, including my knives. I only need your oven and two burners, and a photograph of the kitchen beforehand.

  3. Served at your table

    Each course plated and brought out in its own time. No buffet, no chafing dishes, no queue at a counter.

  4. The kitchen goes back cleaner

    Everything washed, put away and wiped down. You are left at the table for the sobremesa.

Questions

The things people ask before they book

If your question is not here, ask it in the form below and I will answer the same day.

What does a private chef in Gran Canaria cost?

Pricing is quoted per booking, because it depends on which menu you choose, how many are at the table, the date, and where you are on the island. Every quote is all in: menu design, the shopping, all ingredients, the cooking, service at your table and the cleaning up afterwards. Nothing is added on the night. Tell me those four things and you will have a full price the same day.

What services does Alex Chelaru offer as a private chef?

Private dinners in villas and homes, celebrations and private events, barbecue cooked over coals, late breakfast and brunch, weekly meal prep for residents, and multi-day villa or yacht service. Custom menus are written on request for anything not on the standard list, from a tasting for two to canapes for forty.

Does the chef bring the ingredients and clean the kitchen?

Yes to both. I shop that morning, arrive two hours before service with everything including my own knives, cook in your kitchen, serve each course at your table, then wash up and put everything away. You do not shop, plate, pour or wash up at any point.

Do you cater for dietary restrictions, gluten free or vegan?

Yes. Vegetarian and vegan guests are given their own courses rather than the side dishes from everyone else's plate. Gluten free, dairy free, shellfish and nut allergies are all accommodated, and the fourteen EU allergens are declared on the back of every menu card. Tell me at the enquiry stage and the menu is written around it.

Which areas of Gran Canaria do you cover?

Maspalomas, Meloneras, Mogan, Puerto Rico, Playa del Ingles, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria and the villas between them. Anywhere beyond about thirty kilometres carries a small travel supplement. Yachts at Pasito Blanco and Puerto de Mogan by arrangement.

How far in advance should I book a private chef?

At least five days. That is the minimum I need to source properly: the fish, the heritage potatoes, the island cheeses and the kid goat all come from specific suppliers rather than a supermarket. In high season, November to March, earlier is better because dates go quickly.

What do you need from my kitchen?

An oven and two working burners. I bring everything else, including knives, board, thermometer and pans. Villa kitchens are often sparse, which is why I travel with a full kit. A photograph of the kitchen and the table when you book prevents surprises on both sides.

What kind of food do you cook?

European technique, Asian flavour and Canarian ingredients. Cherne with green chilli and coriander mojo, mojo picon, baifo slow cooked with palm honey and star anise, papas antiguas, Flor de Guia cheese set with thistle flower, local prawns with XO butter and gofio. Fish is line caught off Arguineguin and cured the same morning.

What languages do you speak?

Fluent English and native Romanian, with Spanish improving every week. Menus are available in German on request.
